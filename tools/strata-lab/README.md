# Strata lab

Tools for developing Strata, a new artwork (see the vision doc linked from the
session). Write outputs to a scratch folder, not into the repository.

| Tool | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `viewer/` | A self-contained HTML viewer for the land: regions generated and lit in web workers, drawn at a whole number of screen pixels per art pixel (4 by default, as on the TVs) so it looks crisp on any monitor. Pan, zoom, change seeds, jump between biome edges, drift, and mask to the four-screen wall. Build with `node tools/strata-lab/viewer/build.mjs OUT.html --standalone`. |
| `atlas/` | **Strata Atlas**: worlds as maps, from `engine/strata/world2d.ts` (Strata's history in two dimensions: about 100 km of land over sixty eras. The map lies across six to eleven drifting plates: where they press together ranges rise, some old and worn down to hills, some young; where they pull apart rifts sink with lakes along them; some plates are sea floor, so coasts come with bays, inlets and islands; volcanic fields grow their cones and granite domes come to light. Rivers cut down by the stream-power law and branch out by themselves; two or three ice ages send glaciers down the valleys, gouging them and leaving lakes behind moraines; in dry basins water ends in salt flats and salt lakes. The climate is a game world's: every world holds the whole range in a patchwork of some seventy regions about 10 km across (frozen, cold and wet, mild, hot and dry, hot and wet, each several times, and some odd ones), blended through wandering borders, with warmth falling with height and rain wrung out over the mountains on one wind; so ice, pine forest, temperate woods, steppe, desert and jungle all lie within a day's walk, and the days differ in where they lie and in rarer things (volcanoes, salt basins, granite domes), and every place gets its habitat, soil and one of the 65 biomes of `biomes.ts`). Today's world (its seed is the date) and five others, made in workers; biome, climate, height, rain, warmth and rock views; what is under the pointer; a section along a line dragged across the map, showing the strata folded under the ranges. Build with `node tools/strata-lab/atlas/build.mjs OUT.html --standalone`. |
| `vista/` | **Strata Vista**: today's generated world (`engine/strata/world2d.ts`, seeded by the date, made in a worker) at the wall's resolution (980 x 637 art pixels) on the GPU (WebGL2). `land.ts` turns the 200 m world map into land at any point: smooth heights with relief by how rugged it is, rivers traced down the drainage and cut as channels, lakes and the sea at their levels, the biome (borders wandering), what kind of ground that biome makes, and which trees grow there. Places chosen for what lies within a walk (several biomes, a rise and fall of the land, water, rock; one of each kind of climate first, then the best well apart), and one journey through them all (`journey.ts`): a tour from the westmost place, planned over the world map round lakes and the sea, then walked out a few km at a time over the land at full detail (A* by slope, fording rivers only where it must); the 2 m and 16 m grids follow the party, each next placement built by the workers into a second set of textures and swapped in whole, trees hung on the world's cells so they stand where they stood, and faded out by 1.45 km (into the woods' canopy on the ground), always inside the fine grid, so a placement swapped in adds none that can be seen; Go to jumps to where the journey passes a place; in the land, mesas and buttes in the dry country, crags and tors elsewhere, and on rugged ground benches with cliffs where the rock's beds show; the walked line hidden (Path shows it) and kept clear of trees and tall plants; a map of the whole world. Terrain in quadtree patches from three grids (a 2 m and a 16 m grid laid over the place, a 100 m grid over the whole world) built in workers; the ground takes its biome's grass, leaf and sand colours; ground shaded in world-space texels at least an art pixel across, on six-shade ramps; rock faces show their beds; haze in eight bands of distance; every sprite drawn at its exact height in whole pixels (`pack.ts`, made in the workers): trees, shrubs and rocks from 6 px up by whole rows and columns of one drawing, rows in proportion to height (`grow.ts`, tables the shaders read), so each size is the one below with a row or column more and nothing changes its look as it grows; grass, flowers and ferns redrawn at each size (strokes); trees and shrubs near the camera taken apart into groups of leaf clumps, each put back at its own place; anything taller than its tallest drawing drawn in pixels two or three times the size, so it keeps growing; ground cover out to 1.4 km, where even a shrub has shrunk to a pixel and faded by its size (beyond 560 m only what stands 0.6 m or more), made a few tiles a frame; trees of every species the biomes name (`trees.ts`: oak, beech, maple, birch, spruce, fir, larch, Scots and stone pines, feather and fan palms, acacia, juniper, cypress, poplar, weeping willow, olive, baobab, Joshua tree, tree fern, banana, dead snags; the rest drawn as the nearest of these), drawn in materials and shades, so no two trees are coloured alike (16 leaf colours a kind, now and then a broadleaf or larch turning); cacti (saguaro, barrel, prickly pear) where the biome names them; rocks in six families (stones, boulders, slabs, rubble, erratics and crags, 28 drawings; `rocks.ts` draws each as small 3D blocks of stone, lit, grained and cracked), each rock the colour of the bedrock under it, with moss, lichen or snow on its upturned faces by the climate; bushes of the kinds each biome names (bushes, ferns, heather cushions, open sage and agave or yucca rosettes, some yuccas in flower) instead of one shrub everywhere; grasses and flowers of many kinds (short tufts; tall grass with nodding seed heads, as tall as the biome's grass grows; sedge; reeds with cattails or plumes along reedy shores; moss; daisies, lupin and foxglove spikes, cow-parsley umbels, poppy cups and alpine cushions, by climate), the flowers in 16 colours a biome (its own bloom varied, with white, yellow and a companion colour), each plant its own; grass, bushes and ferns in their biome's colours. Speeds up to 250x, a slider along the route, 10 and 100 m steps and frame stepping. Camera shots: Follow (looks ahead into the land, turning again where the way turns away), Trail (close behind the party, looking the way they go) and Turn; Auto follows, and drops into Trail while trees or a hill would hide the party (tested against the trees and the land between), whole-pixel camera snapping, a window through things that stand in front of the party, and a frame-time and GPU readout. Today's land and the sprites are saved in the browser (`cache.ts`, IndexedDB, about 7 MB and 75 MB) so a later visit skips making them: each is used only if made by the same code (`build.mjs` hashes the bundled code that makes the land into `LAND_CODE`, and that which draws and packs the sprites into `SPRITE_CODE`; a change there makes it again), the land only for the same day and world size, anything else is deleted, and Forget saved world empties both. Paused, the page draws nothing until something changes the picture. Build with `node tools/strata-lab/vista/build.mjs OUT.html --standalone`; `window.vista.hold()` and `.step(dt)` drive it frame by frame for checks under software GL. |
| `land-render.ts` | The lab's land renderer (sky, strata, cave light), shared by the viewer and `regions.ts`. |
| `regions.ts` | Renders land from the region generator (`engine/strata/generate.ts`): a four-region panorama, wall-sized close-ups of each region edge, and a 12-seed contact sheet (skip it with `--quick`). Prints generation time, scaled to the mini PC. |
| `pi-budget/` | Estimates frame cost on the target machine from this one (`--target 780m` for the Ryzen 7 / Radeon 780M mini PC, `pi5` for a Raspberry Pi 5): CPU time scaled by a calibration factor from published benchmarks, GPU time from a cost model. Spikes for the material simulation and the light flood. See its README. |
| `sketches/motion.py` | A 20-second motion test of the cavern panel, with light options (stepped, steady, calm, banded, smooth) and a count of pixels the light alone changes per frame. |
| `sketches/mockup.py` | A static mock-up of one dusk tableau on the default four-screen wall, used to settle the look: pixel size, light, palette. Needs numpy and Pillow. |

```sh
node --import tsx tools/strata-lab/regions.ts /tmp/strata-land 7 --quick   # seed 7
python tools/strata-lab/sketches/mockup.py /tmp/strata 320 480 640   # pixel sizes
python tools/strata-lab/sketches/mockup.py /tmp/strata --variants   # looks at 480
```

The look variants (`VARIANTS` in the script) are a dark cut-away with pools of
light, a brighter illustrated cross-section, smooth instead of stepped light,
a warm sandstone day, and a cold granite night.

Each size is the pixel-art width of one 531 mm panel (480 = 4×4 screen pixels
per art pixel on a 1080p panel). It writes the full wall at screen resolution,
a 1600-pixel preview, and a 1:1 crop around the camp.
