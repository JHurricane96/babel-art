# Strata lab

Tools for developing Strata, a new artwork (see the vision doc linked from the
session). Write outputs to a scratch folder, not into the repository.

| Tool | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `viewer/` | A self-contained HTML viewer for the land: regions generated and lit in web workers, drawn at a whole number of screen pixels per art pixel (4 by default, as on the TVs) so it looks crisp on any monitor. Pan, zoom, change seeds, jump between biome edges, drift, and mask to the four-screen wall. **Journey** (prototype) sends a party of four along the regions' routes (`engine/strata/route.ts`) with the camera following, at 1×, 10× or 50×; **Route** shows the route. Ladders, ropes, bridges and dug tunnels and shafts are drawn on the overlay where the route needs them; nothing is built into the world yet. Build with `node tools/strata-lab/viewer/build.mjs OUT.html --standalone`. |
| `world/` | The world overview: builds one world's history (`engine/strata/history.ts`: a finite world stepped through 48 eras of uplift, folding, rifting, volcanoes, climate, rivers, erosion and deposition) and draws it: climate along the world, the whole world in cross-section with a draggable close-up, crossing rivers and faults, and an era slider that replays history. Build with `node tools/strata-lab/world/build.mjs OUT.html --standalone`. |
| `atlas/` | **Strata Atlas**: worlds as maps, from `engine/strata/world2d.ts` (Strata's history in two dimensions: about 100 km of land over sixty eras. The map lies across six to eleven drifting plates: where they press together ranges rise, some old and worn down to hills, some young; where they pull apart rifts sink with lakes along them; some plates are sea floor, so coasts come with bays, inlets and islands; volcanic fields grow their cones and granite domes come to light. Rivers cut down by the stream-power law and branch out by themselves; two or three ice ages send glaciers down the valleys, gouging them and leaving lakes behind moraines; in dry basins water ends in salt flats and salt lakes. The climate is a game world's: warmth changes across the map as well as with height, rain comes on one wind and dries out inland and behind the mountains, some worlds have dry summers or dry winters; so one map holds several climate zones, and every place gets its habitat, soil and one of the 65 biomes of `biomes.ts`). Today's world (its seed is the date) and five others, made in workers; biome, climate, height, rain, warmth and rock views; what is under the pointer; a section along a line dragged across the map, showing the strata folded under the ranges. Build with `node tools/strata-lab/atlas/build.mjs OUT.html --standalone`. |
| `vista/` | **Strata Vista**: the layered valley (a hand-shaped test place: a river valley, a hill town, switchbacks to a pass, a lake beyond) at the wall's resolution (980 x 637 art pixels) on the GPU (WebGL2). Terrain in quadtree patches from three grids (2 m, 16 m, 96 m) built in workers; ground shaded in world-space texels at least an art pixel across, on six-shade ramps; rock faces show their beds; haze in eight bands of distance; houses as real geometry with pixel-art walls and roofs; every sprite drawn at its exact height in whole pixels (`pack.ts`, made in the workers): small ones from images made for each height, bigger ones by whole rows and columns of one drawing (`grow.ts`, tables the shaders read), and trees and shrubs near the camera taken apart into groups of leaf clumps, each put back at its own place, so they grow evenly; drawn in materials and shades, so no two trees are coloured alike (16 leaf colours a kind, now and then a tree turning). Speeds up to 250x, a slider along the route, 10 and 100 m steps and frame stepping. A camera director (Auto picks Follow or Turn by how straight the route ahead runs), whole-pixel camera snapping, a window through things that stand in front of the party, and a frame-time and GPU readout. Build with `node tools/strata-lab/vista/build.mjs OUT.html --standalone`; `window.vista.hold()` and `.step(dt)` drive it frame by frame for checks under software GL. |
| `lookbook.ts` | Renders a fixed set of places (one per landform and the key biomes) as viewer-like stills in Node, several processes at a time, into contact sheets; `--before DIR` renders another checkout too and pairs each place. Each still is checked automatically (streaks in the rock, very dark masses, ground pressed flat at the sky's ceiling, framing) and flagged stills get a red frame. 20 places take about 7 s. `node --import tsx tools/strata-lab/lookbook.ts OUT_DIR [--before DIR] [--only 1,5] [--spots FILE]`. |
| `probe.ts` | Everything that made one place, layer by layer: the world's column, landform, climate, habitat, soil and biome, ground and water, rock beds, the generated column as runs of material, and the renderer's depth and light; `--png` writes a zoomed crop. `node --import tsx tools/strata-lab/probe.ts SEED X [Y] [--png OUT.png]`. |
| `world-cache.ts` | A disk cache of world histories (under `node_modules/.cache/strata-worlds`, keyed to the engine's code) for the lookbook, the probe and the generation tests. |
| `land-render.ts` | The lab's land renderer (sky, strata, cave light), shared by the viewer and `regions.ts`. |
| `regions.ts` | Renders land from the region generator (`engine/strata/generate.ts`): a four-region panorama, wall-sized close-ups of each region edge, and a 12-seed contact sheet (skip it with `--quick`). Prints generation time, scaled to the mini PC. |
| `pi-budget/` | Estimates frame cost on the target machine from this one (`--target 780m` for the Ryzen 7 / Radeon 780M mini PC, `pi5` for a Raspberry Pi 5): CPU time scaled by a calibration factor from published benchmarks, GPU time from a cost model. Spikes for the material simulation and the light flood. See its README. |
| `sketches/motion.py` | A 20-second motion test of the cavern panel, with light options (stepped, steady, calm, banded, smooth) and a count of pixels the light alone changes per frame. |
| `sketches/mockup.py` | A static mock-up of one dusk tableau on the default four-screen wall, used to settle the look: pixel size, light, palette. Needs numpy and Pillow. |

```sh
node --import tsx tools/strata-lab/regions.ts /tmp/strata-land 7 --quick   # seed 7
python tools/strata-lab/sketches/mockup.py /tmp/strata 320 480 640   # pixel sizes
python tools/strata-lab/sketches/mockup.py /tmp/strata --variants   # looks at 480
```

The look variants (`VARIANTS` in the script) are a dark cut-away with pools of
light, a brighter illustrated cross-section, smooth instead of stepped light,
a warm sandstone day, and a cold granite night.

Each size is the pixel-art width of one 531 mm panel (480 = 4×4 screen pixels
per art pixel on a 1080p panel). It writes the full wall at screen resolution,
a 1600-pixel preview, and a 1:1 crop around the camp.

Tests: `npm run test:strata` (about 12 s with the world cache warm) or `npm run test:strata:quick` (determinism, seams, water, budget: about 6 s).
